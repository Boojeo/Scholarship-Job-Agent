# Guide: what a great SOP and recommendation letter contain, questions to think with, and which documents you need

## Part 1. The Statement of Purpose (SOP)

### What committees look for
They read your SOP to answer one question: **"Will this person succeed in our programme and do good research?"** Every paragraph should help them answer "yes".

### The structure that works (about 800–1,000 words)
| # | Paragraph | Its job | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** | One concrete moment or question that shows how you think. Not "Since childhood I loved computers". | 3–5 sentences |
| 2 | **Foundation** | Grades and training *as evidence*, briefly (4.99, KAUST Academy). | short |
| 3–4 | **Research stories** | For each project: question → what *you* did → result (numbers) → what you learned. This is the core. | 60% of the text |
| 5 | **Why this programme** | 2–3 named professors, labs or courses and the exact link to your work. Rewritten for every university. | 1 paragraph |
| 6 | **Beyond research** | Leadership and teaching, in 2–3 sentences, and what it shows about you. | short |
| 7 | **Goals** | What you will do after the degree, and how this programme leads there. | 2–3 sentences |

### The 8 rules
1. **Show, don't claim.** Not "I am passionate about AI" but "I ran 37,800 experiments on free GPU quotas".
2. **Say "I", and be precise about your role** in group work ("I built…", "I designed…").
3. **Each project ends with a lesson.** That reflection is what separates a strong SOP from a CV in paragraphs.
4. **One idea connects everything.** Yours: *making LLMs cheaper to run and easier to trust*.
5. **Name professors correctly** and mention one of their recent papers.
6. **No sad story unless it shaped your research direction.** Put personal background in the Personal History statement instead.
7. **Respect the word limit.** If none is given, aim for 1–2 pages.
8. **Have someone outside AI read it.** If they can't say what you want to research, simplify.

### Questions to think about (write short answers; I'll turn them into the SOP)
**For the opening (pick the strongest true answer):**
1. What moment in your experiments surprised you most? What did you see, and what did you think?
2. When did you first realise that LLMs can be confidently wrong? What happened?
3. Was there a moment when lack of compute or data almost stopped you? What did you do?
4. Why Arabic dialects? Did something happen with family, friends or users that made you care?

**For the research stories:**
5. On Periscope: what exactly did *you* do? Which code, which experiments, which idea came from you?
6. On the multi-agent study: whose idea was it? What was the hardest bug or design decision?
7. On Lahja: what was the most surprising result? What did the demo users tell you?
8. What would you do next on each project if you had a year and a GPU cluster?

**For goals and fit:**
9. In 10 years, where do you want to be: a professor, an industry research lab, or building a company? In which country or region?
10. What problem do you want to be known for solving?
11. Which 3 professors' papers have you read and liked? (Send me their names and I'll write the "Why this programme" paragraphs.)
12. Do you prefer an MS with a thesis, or going straight to a PhD? Why?

**For the personal-history statement:**
13. What made you choose AI and computer science?
14. What has being a Somali student in Saudi Arabia, learning in English, given you that others might not have?
15. Who helped you most on this path, and how do you help others now?

## Part 2. A strong recommendation letter

### What makes a letter strong (in order of importance)
1. **Comparison:** "the best student I have taught in 10 years" or "top 1% of 17,000 applicants". This is the line committees look for first.
2. **Specific stories** that only this person could tell: "When his experiment failed, he…".
3. **Evidence of independence:** "He proposed the idea himself", "He needed little supervision".
4. **Research potential,** not only good grades.
5. **Character:** reliability, integrity, how you handle feedback and pressure.
6. **Context:** how long and in what role they know you, and why their judgment is credible.
7. **A clear closing:** "I recommend him without reservation / with the highest enthusiasm."

**Weak letters** only repeat the CV, are generic ("hard-working, punctual"), or are short. A letter from a famous person who barely knows you is weaker than a detailed letter from someone who supervised you.

### Questions to answer for each recommender (put them in the brag sheet)
1. When and how did they first meet you?
2. What did you do in front of them that impressed them? Give 2 concrete moments.
3. Which skill did they see best: research, coding, leadership, teaching?
4. Did you ever handle a setback or criticism in front of them? What did you do?
5. Is there a number they can state, such as a rank in their class or "best of N students"?
